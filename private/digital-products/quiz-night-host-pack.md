# Quiz Night Host Pack
**DoyinTech · 139 questions in 8 rounds, with answers, scorecard and host script**

Everything you need to run a fun quiz for a church social, school event, office party, birthday or family gathering. Questions run from easy (*) to hard (***), so children and adults can play together.

---

## How to run your quiz night (host script)
**Before the night**
- Choose 4–6 rounds (about 10 minutes each). A two-hour event fits 6 rounds with a break.
- Print one answer sheet per team per round and one scorecard for the scorer.
- Teams of 3–6 work best. Mix ages so everyone can help.
- Prepare prizes: one for the winners, one for the best team name, one for last place.

**Opening (read aloud)**
"Good evening everyone, and welcome to quiz night! Tonight there are [NUMBER] rounds of questions. Write your team name on every answer sheet. No phones, no searching online: honesty makes it fun! I'll read each question twice. At the end of each round, swap sheets with the team next to you for marking. Let's begin with Round 1: [ROUND NAME]."

**During each round**
- Read each question twice, slowly, with its number.
- Give 30 seconds per question; announce "last 10 seconds".
- After the round, read the answers and the fun facts. People love the facts.
- Collect sheets and update the scorecard. Announce the top three teams after each round.

**Tie-breaker**
Ask one number question: "In metres, how high is Mount Kilimanjaro?" (Answer: about 5,895.) The closest team wins.

**Closing**
"Thank you all for playing! Congratulations to [WINNING TEAM]. A big round of applause for everyone, and thank you to [HOST/ORGANISERS]."

# Answers and fun facts
## Round 1: Nigeria
1. **c) Abuja** – Abuja replaced Lagos as the capital in 1991. Lagos is still the biggest city.
2. **b) 1960** – Independence came on 1 October 1960. Nigeria became a republic in 1963.
3. **b) Green and white** – Green for the land and white for peace. It was designed by Michael Taiwo Akinkunmi.
4. **b) Naira** – One naira is made up of 100 kobo.
5. **c) 36** – 36 states plus the Federal Capital Territory, Abuja.
6. **a) Lagos** – Lagos is one of the largest cities in Africa.
7. **a) Nigeria** – Nollywood makes more films each year than almost any other industry in the world.
8. **a) Yoruba** – Hausa is widely spoken in the north and Igbo in the south-east.
9. **c) Africa** – Over 200 million people live in Nigeria.
10. **b) A colourful printed fabric** – Ankara is wax-print cotton, used for everything from dresses to bags. Ankara is also the capital of Turkey!
11. **b) A dry, dusty wind from the Sahara** – The Harmattan usually blows from about November to March, bringing cool mornings and hazy skies.
12. **a) River Niger** – The Niger flows through Nigeria to the Atlantic. The Benue is its largest tributary.
13. **c) Lokoja** – Lokoja, the capital of Kogi State, sits where the two great rivers join.
14. **d) Chinua Achebe** – Published in 1958, it is one of the most widely read African novels ever.
15. **c) Wole Soyinka** – Wole Soyinka won it in 1986.
16. **c) Abeokuta** – Olumo Rock once gave the Egba people shelter in times of war.
17. **a) Gulf of Guinea** – The Gulf of Guinea is part of the Atlantic Ocean.
18. **d) Ghana** – Nigeria borders Benin, Niger, Chad and Cameroon. Ghana is further west.
19. **b) Nigeria, We Hail Thee** – Nigeria brought back its 1960 anthem in 2024, replacing 'Arise, O Compatriots', used since 1978.
20. **d) Abuja** – The huge rock is just outside Abuja, in Niger State, and appears on the ₦100 note.
21. **c) Cross River** – It sits on the Obudu Plateau, high enough to be cool all year.
22. **a) Bauchi** – Yankari is famous for elephants and the warm Wikki Springs.
23. **d) Chappal Waddi** – Chappal Waddi, in Taraba State, is about 2,400 metres high.
24. **c) River Niger** – Kainji is one of Nigeria's main hydroelectric power stations.
25. **a) Unity and Faith, Peace and Progress** – The motto appears on the coat of arms.
26. **c) 1991** – The move was planned from 1976 and completed in December 1991.

## Round 3: Science
1. **c) Mercury** – A year on Mercury is just 88 Earth days.
2. **c) Mars** – Rusty iron in its soil makes Mars look red.
3. **c) Jupiter** – More than 1,000 Earths could fit inside Jupiter.
4. **a) 100 °C** – That is 212 °F. On a high mountain, water boils at a lower temperature.
5. **c) H₂O** – Two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom.
6. **d) Carbon dioxide** – Plants use carbon dioxide and sunlight to make food, and give out oxygen.
7. **a) 6** – Spiders have 8, so they are not insects.
8. **c) About 365 days** – The extra quarter day is why we have leap years.
9. **d) 0 °C** – That is 32 °F.
10. **a) Gravity** – Gravity also keeps the Moon going around Earth.
11. **c) Mosquito** – Only female Anopheles mosquitoes spread it. Sleeping under a treated net helps.
12. **a) Saturn** – Jupiter, Uranus and Neptune have faint rings too.
13. **d) Honey** – Bees also make beeswax, and they pollinate many of our crops.
14. **a) Skin** – An adult's skin weighs around 4 kg.
15. **d) 206** – Babies are born with nearly 300; some join together as they grow.
16. **d) Diamond** – Diamond is pure carbon, the same element as pencil graphite.
17. **b) Vitamin D** – Vitamin D helps keep bones strong.
18. **a) Nucleus** – The nucleus holds protons and neutrons; electrons move around it.
19. **b) White blood cells** – Red cells carry oxygen; platelets help blood clot.
20. **a) Albert Einstein** – His famous equation is E = mc².
21. **b) 300,000 km per second** – Sunlight takes about 8 minutes to reach Earth.
22. **c) Solids, like steel** – Sound cannot travel through empty space at all.
