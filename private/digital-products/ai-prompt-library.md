# AI Prompt Library for Small Business

130 ready-to-use prompts for ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot and other AI assistants, in 13 categories. Your download also includes **ai-prompt-library.xlsx**, a searchable version that fills in your business details automatically.

## How to get great answers

Every strong prompt has five parts:

1. **Role:** who the AI should act as ("You are an experienced bookkeeper for small businesses").
2. **Task:** exactly what you want ("Write 3 payment reminder messages").
3. **Details:** the facts it needs: your business, customer, price, deadline.
4. **Format:** how the answer should look: a table, a list, under 100 words.
5. **Rules:** what to avoid: jargon, made-up numbers, a pushy tone.

Start each new chat by pasting a short summary of your business, then use the prompts below. Replace everything in [square brackets] with your own details.

**Business summary to paste first:**

> About my business: [business] sells [product] to [audience] in [location]. Write in a [tone] tone. Keep answers practical and specific to my business. Ask me questions if you need more detail, and tell me when you are unsure of a fact.

## Good habits

- Never paste passwords, card numbers or private customer data into an AI tool.
- AI can be confidently wrong. Check facts, numbers, prices and anything legal, tax or medical before you publish or act.
- Edit the result so it sounds like you. AI gives you a fast first draft.
- The first answer is rarely the best. Use the last category, "Prompts that improve any answer", to sharpen it.

## Marketing & social media

### 1. 30-day content calendar

*Use it when:* You need a month of posts and don't know where to start

> You are a social media strategist for [business], which sells [product] to [audience] in [location]. Create a 30-day content calendar for [platform]. Mix 40% helpful tips, 25% behind the scenes, 20% customer proof and 15% direct offers. For each day give: the post idea, a hook for the first line, the format (reel, carousel, photo, story or text) and a call to action. Return it as a table. Tone: [tone].

### 2. Hooks that stop the scroll

*Use it when:* Your posts get few views

> Write 20 opening lines for short videos or posts about [topic] for [audience]. Use different angles: a surprising fact, a common mistake, a bold claim, a question, a quick story, a before-and-after, a number list. Each hook under 12 words. No clickbait that the content can't deliver. Mark your top 5 and say why.

### 3. Repurpose one piece into ten

*Use it when:* You made one good post or video and want more from it

> Here is a piece of content I made: [paste content]. Turn it into: 1 carousel (slide-by-slide text, 8 slides max), 3 short-video scripts under 40 seconds, 2 text posts for LinkedIn or Facebook, 3 tweets/threads posts and 1 email to my list. Keep the core message, adapt the style to each platform, and keep [tone] tone.

### 4. Instagram/TikTok bio

*Use it when:* Your profile doesn't say clearly what you do

> Write 5 profile bio options for [business] on [platform]. Each must say who we help, what we offer and why to follow or buy, in under 150 characters, with one clear call to action that points to the link. Audience: [audience] in [location]. Tone: [tone].

### 5. Promotion plan for a launch

*Use it when:* You are launching a product, service or sale

> I'm launching [offer] for [business] on [date] at [price]. Audience: [audience]. Build a 14-day launch plan: 7 days of warm-up content, launch day, and 6 days of follow-through. For each day list the channel (social, email, WhatsApp/SMS, ads), the message, and the goal. Include 3 objection-handling posts and a last-chance message. Keep it realistic for a team of [team size].

### 6. Ad copy variations

*Use it when:* You are about to run paid ads

> Write 6 ad variations for [platform] promoting [product] to [audience] in [location]. For each: primary text (under 125 characters), headline (under 40 characters), and description. Use different angles: pain point, desired outcome, social proof, offer/urgency, objection, curiosity. Avoid claims we can't prove. Suggest which 2 to test first and why.

### 7. Customer persona

*Use it when:* You're not sure who exactly you're talking to

> Based on this information about our customers: [paste what you know: reviews, common questions, who buys], create 2 customer personas for [business]. For each: a name and short background, goals, frustrations, what they search for or ask, objections before buying, where they spend time online, and the words they use. End with 5 marketing messages each persona would respond to.

### 8. Caption with a call to action

*Use it when:* You have a photo or video and need the words

> Write 3 captions for this post: [describe the photo/video]. Business: [business]. Goal of the post: [goal: sales, comments, saves, DMs]. Each caption: a strong first line, 2–4 short lines of value or story, and one clear call to action. Add 5 relevant hashtags for [location]. Tone: [tone].

### 9. Competitor content review

*Use it when:* You want to learn from what works for others

> Here are posts or ads from competitors: [paste text or describe them]. Analyse them: which themes, hooks and offers they repeat, what their audience responds to, and what they ignore. Then give me 10 content ideas that [business] can do better or differently, based on our strengths: [list strengths].

### 10. Influencer outreach message

*Use it when:* You want creators to promote your product

> Write a short, friendly first message to a [niche] creator with [follower range] followers, asking them to try [product] from [business]. Mention something specific about their content ([detail]), what we'd offer ([free product / fee / commission]), and a simple next step. Under 90 words. Also write a polite follow-up for 5 days later.

Give me 5 ways to respond that keep the price but show value: break down cost per use or per day, compare with the cost of the problem, offer a smaller package, payment plan, or a guarantee. For each, write the exact reply in [tone] tone. Tell me which fits best and why.

### 14. Sales call script

Sections: rapport (2 min), questions to understand their situation and goals (8 questions), summarising what I heard, presenting the solution tied to their answers, price, handling 3 likely objections ([list them]), and closing with a clear next step. Keep the questions open-ended.

### 15. Upsell and cross-sell ideas

### 16. Cold outreach message

### 17. Win back old customers

### 18. Sales page outline

*Use it when:* You need a page that sells one offer

Write the full copy for the headline, problem and FAQ sections.

### 19. Pricing your packages

*Use it when:* You don't know how to structure your offers

> I sell [service] to [audience]. My costs per job are about [cost] and competitors charge [range]. Design 3 packages (basic, standard, premium) with what's included in each, a suggested price, and why the middle one should be the most popular. Include one add-on. ### 20. Testimonials request

*Use it when:* You need more reviews and case studies

> Write a message asking a happy customer of [business] for a testimonial. Also write a version asking for a Google review with a link placeholder.

## Customer service

### 27. Onboarding message for new customers

### 28. Delay or out-of-stock notice

*Use it when:* You have to deliver bad news

> Write a message telling customers their order of [product] is delayed by [time] because [reason]. Be honest and brief, give the new date, offer options (wait, swap, refund), and add a small goodwill gesture ([gesture]). Under 100 words.
