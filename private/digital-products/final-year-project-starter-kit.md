# Final Year Project Starter Kit
**DoyinTech · for university and polytechnic students in Nigeria**

Templates and checklists for every stage of your project: choosing a topic, the proposal, Chapters 1–5, the questionnaire, references and the defence. Follow your department's handbook wherever it differs.

---

## Stage 1 — Choosing a topic (week 1–2)

A good topic is **narrow, researchable in your time and money, and interesting to your supervisor**.

Formula: **[Effect/Impact/Assessment/Design] of [X] on [Y] in [specific place/organisation]**
- *Impact of mobile banking on customer satisfaction in selected banks in Jos Metropolis*
- *Design and implementation of a web-based hostel allocation system for [University]*
- *Assessment of waste management practices among market traders in Ogbete Main Market, Enugu*

Check before you submit three topics:
- [ ] Can I get the data (respondents, records, software) within 6 weeks?
- [ ] Are there at least 15 recent studies (last 10 years) on the subject?
- [ ] Is the place or population specific enough?
- [ ] Can I explain it in one sentence?

## Stage 2 — Proposal template (2–5 pages)

1. **Title**
2. **Background** — 2–3 paragraphs from general to specific.
3. **Statement of the problem** — what is wrong, evidence it is wrong, what happens if nothing is done.
4. **Aim and objectives** — one aim; 3–5 objectives beginning with *to examine / to determine / to assess / to design*.
5. **Research questions or hypotheses** — one for each objective.
6. **Scope** — place, population, period.
7. **Methodology** — design, population, sample size, instrument, method of analysis.
8. **References**

## Stage 3 — Chapter templates

### Chapter One: Introduction
1.1 Background to the study
1.2 Statement of the problem
1.3 Aim and objectives of the study
1.4 Research questions
1.5 Research hypotheses (if quantitative)
1.6 Significance of the study — who benefits and how
1.7 Scope and limitations
1.8 Definition of terms
1.9 (Optional) Organisation of the study

### Chapter Two: Literature review
2.1 Conceptual review — define each key variable using 2–3 authors each.
2.2 Theoretical framework — one or two theories, author and year, how they explain your study.
2.3 Empirical review — summarise 10–20 studies: **author (year), aim, method, findings, gap**.
2.4 Summary and gap in literature — what is missing that your study fills.

Empirical review table (draft it here first):
| Author (year) | Aim | Location | Method | Findings | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

### Chapter Three: Methodology
3.1 Research design (survey, experimental, case study, descriptive, system development)
3.2 Population of the study
3.3 Sample size and sampling technique (Taro Yamane: n = N ÷ (1 + N·e²), e = 0.05)
3.4 Instrument for data collection
3.5 Validity and reliability of the instrument (pilot test, Cronbach's alpha ≥ 0.7)
3.6 Method of data collection
3.7 Method of data analysis (frequencies, percentages, mean, chi-square, regression, t-test)

*For computer science/engineering:* 3.1 Analysis of the existing system · 3.2 Problems of the existing system · 3.3 Proposed system · 3.4 System design (use case, flowchart, ERD, database tables) · 3.5 Tools and languages.

### Chapter Four: Data presentation, analysis and discussion
4.1 Response rate (questionnaires distributed vs returned)
4.2 Demographic data
4.3 Analysis by research question (table + interpretation under each)
4.4 Test of hypotheses (state H0, test used, result, decision)
4.5 Discussion of findings — compare with authors from Chapter Two

*For system projects:* 4.1 System requirements · 4.2 Implementation · 4.3 Testing (test cases table) · 4.4 Screenshots with captions.

### Chapter Five: Summary, conclusion and recommendations
5.1 Summary — one paragraph per chapter
5.2 Conclusion — answer each research question in one or two sentences
5.3 Recommendations — tied to findings, addressed to specific people
5.4 Contribution to knowledge
5.5 Suggestions for further study

## Stage 5 — References (APA 7th)
- Book: Okafor, C. (2021). *Title of book in italics*. Publisher.
- Journal: Bello, A., & Musa, T. (2023). Article title. *Journal Name, 12*(3), 45–60. https://doi.org/xxxx
- Website: Central Bank of Nigeria. (2024). *Title of page*. https://www.cbn.gov.ng/...
- In text: (Okafor, 2021) · Okafor (2021) found that…

Use Zotero or Mendeley (free) from day one, or our citation tool at doyintech.com/students/citations.

## Stage 6 — Formatting checklist
- [ ] Times New Roman 12, double line spacing (or as your department specifies)
- [ ] Margins: 1.5" left, 1" others
- [ ] Preliminary pages: title, certification, dedication, acknowledgement, abstract (150–300 words), table of contents, list of tables/figures
- [ ] Tables numbered by chapter (Table 4.1) with titles above
- [ ] Figures numbered with captions below
- [ ] Page numbers: Roman for preliminary pages, Arabic from Chapter One
- [ ] Plagiarism check before submission

---

## Stage 7 — Defence preparation
- 10–12 slides: title, problem, objectives, methodology, key findings (2–3 slides), conclusion, recommendations, thank you.
- Rehearse to 10 minutes with a timer.
- Prepare answers to the questions panels always ask:
  1. Why this topic?
  2. How did you arrive at your sample size?
  3. Why this method of analysis?
  4. What are the limitations?
  5. What is your contribution to knowledge?
  6. If you did it again, what would you change?
- Bring three printed copies and a backup on a flash drive and email.
