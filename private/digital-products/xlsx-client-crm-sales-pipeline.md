# Client CRM & Sales Pipeline: quick-start guide

Your download includes **client-crm-sales-pipeline.xlsx**. It works in Excel, Google Sheets, LibreOffice and Numbers, in any currency.

## What's inside

| Sheet | What it does |
|---|---|
| Pipeline | Deals and value per stage, open pipeline, weighted forecast, won value, win rate, average won deal and overdue follow-ups |
| Settings | Sales stages with the chance each one closes, lead sources and salespeople |
| Contacts | Everyone you might sell to. "Days since" last contact turns amber after 30 days |
| Deals | Each opportunity with owner, stage, value, expected close date and next follow-up. Alerts show overdue follow-ups |

## Set it up

1. **Settings:** keep the default stages or rename them to match how you sell. Adjust the "chance to close" for each stage once you have a few months of real results. Keep "Won" and "Lost" spelled exactly as they are.
2. Add your lead sources and the names of anyone who sells.
3. Delete the example contacts and deals, then add your real ones.

## The daily 10-minute routine

1. Open **Deals** and filter or sort by **Next follow-up**.
2. Work the red rows first (follow-up overdue), then amber (due today or no date set).
3. After each conversation, update the stage, set a new follow-up date, and write one line in Notes.
4. On **Contacts**, update "Last contact" for anyone you spoke to.

## What the numbers mean

- **Weighted value** = deal value × chance to close. Adding these up gives a realistic forecast instead of a hopeful one.
- **Win rate** = deals won ÷ deals closed (won + lost). If it's low, look at which stage deals die in.
- **Open pipeline** compared with your monthly target tells you if you need more leads (pipeline too small) or better follow-up (pipeline big, few wins).

## Follow-up rules that win more deals

- Every open deal has a next follow-up date. No date means no deal.
- Follow up on a sent proposal within 2 working days.
- Most sales need several touches. Vary them: a message, a useful link, a call.
- Mark a deal Lost when it's lost, and note why. Patterns in lost reasons are gold.
