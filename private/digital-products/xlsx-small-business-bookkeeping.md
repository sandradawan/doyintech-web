# Small Business & Freelancer Bookkeeping: quick-start guide

Your download includes **small-business-bookkeeping.xlsx**. It works in Excel, Google Sheets, LibreOffice and Numbers, in any currency.

## What's inside

| Sheet | What it does |
|---|---|
| Summary | Income, expenses, profit, tax to set aside and margin for each month and the year; money owed to you; spending by category |
| Settings | Business name, year, tax set-aside %, income and expense categories |
| Invoices | Every invoice you send. Unpaid invoices past their due date turn red and show the days overdue |
| Income | Money actually received |
| Expenses | Everything the business spends, with a receipt reference column |

## Set it up

1. **Settings:** enter your business name, the year, and the share of profit you want to set aside for tax (25% is filled in as a starting point).
2. Edit the category lists to match your business. Keep expense categories close to the ones your accountant or tax form uses; it makes year-end much faster.
3. Delete the example rows on Invoices, Income and Expenses.

## The weekly 15-minute routine

1. **Invoices:** add any invoice you sent. Mark paid ones as Paid with the date.
2. **Income:** log money received this week. When an invoice is paid, add it here too (the Invoices sheet tracks what is owed, the Income sheet tracks cash in).
3. **Expenses:** log spending, and type the receipt number or file name in the reference column.
4. **Summary:** check "Money owed to you". Chase anything red the same day.

## Getting paid faster

- Put the due date on every invoice. 7 or 14 days is normal for small jobs.
- For new clients, ask for a 30–50% deposit before you start.
- Send a polite reminder the day after the due date, then again at 7 and 14 days. Anything over 30 days late needs a phone call.
- The **Oldest overdue (days)** number on Summary is your early warning.

## About the tax set-aside

The sheet moves a percentage of each month's profit into a "Tax to set aside" column so tax day is never a shock. Move that amount to a separate savings account each month. The percentage is a savings guide, not tax advice: rates and rules depend on your country and business type, so confirm your real rate with a local accountant.

## Tips

- Cells shaded light orange are for typing. The rest calculates.
- Each month on Summary comes from the date you type, so always fill in dates.
- At year end, save a copy, rename it with the year, clear the entry rows and change the year on Settings.
