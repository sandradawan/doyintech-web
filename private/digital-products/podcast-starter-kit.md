# Podcast Starter Kit
**DoyinTech · Plan, record and publish your first episodes with the phone you already own**

You don't need a studio to start. You need a clear topic, a quiet room and a simple routine you can keep up. This kit takes you from idea to your first three published episodes.

---

## Part 1: Plan your show
Answer these before you record anything. Write short answers.

1. **Who is this show for?** Name one kind of person, for example "Nigerian fashion designers selling on Instagram".
2. **What problem or interest brings them to you?**
3. **What will they get from each episode?** One useful idea, one good story or one honest conversation.
4. **Why you?** Your experience, your job, your curiosity or your access to interesting people.
5. **What is the show called?** Make it easy to say, spell and search for. Say what it's about if you can.
6. **How long is each episode?** 15–30 minutes is a good start. Pick what you can finish.
7. **How often?** Weekly or every two weeks. A steady schedule beats a busy first month.

**Your one-line pitch:**
"[Show name] helps [who] to [result], with [format] every [day]."

**Ten-episode test:** list ten episode ideas now. If you can't reach ten, narrow or change the topic.

## Part 2: Your equipment
Start with level 1. Upgrade only when you've published at least five episodes.

**Level 1: phone only**
- Your phone's voice recorder.
- Hold the phone a hand's width from your mouth, slightly to the side, so breath sounds don't hit the microphone.
- Turn on airplane mode so calls don't interrupt you.

**Level 2: add a clip-on microphone**
- A wired or wireless lavalier microphone that fits your phone's port.
- This is the cheapest big improvement in sound.

**Level 3: USB microphone**
- A dynamic USB microphone for a laptop picks up less room noise than a condenser microphone, which helps in a noisy area.
- Add a foam windscreen and any headphones.

**Make any room sound better**
- Record in a small room with soft things in it: clothes, curtains, a bed, cushions. A wardrobe full of clothes works very well.
- Turn off fans and air conditioning while recording.
- Record late at night or early in the morning if your street is noisy, and before the generators start if you can.
- Always charge your devices first, and record 30 seconds as a test before you begin.

## Part 3: Episode scripts

### Solo episode (15–20 minutes)
- **Hook (30 seconds):** "By the end of this episode you'll know how to..."
- **Intro (20 seconds):** "This is [show name], the show that helps [who] to [result]. I'm [your name]."
- **Why this matters (2 minutes):** one short real story.
- **Point 1 (4 minutes):** the idea, an example, what to do.
- **Point 2 (4 minutes):** the idea, an example, what to do.
- **Point 3 (4 minutes):** the idea, an example, what to do.
- **Recap (1 minute):** "So remember three things..."
- **Call to action (30 seconds):** one request only. "Send this to one friend who needs it."

### Interview episode (25–40 minutes)
- **Hook:** the best line from the conversation, added when you edit.
- **Introduce your guest:** who they are and why listeners should care, in two sentences.
- **Ask these, in this order:**
  1. How did you get started?
  2. What was the hardest moment, and what did you do?
  3. What do most people get wrong about this?
  4. Can you take us through exactly how you do [skill]?
  5. What would you tell someone starting today?
  6. Where can people find you?
- **Close:** thank the guest and give your one call to action.

**Interview tips:** send the questions ahead of time. Listen more than you talk. "Tell me more about that" is often your best question.

### Message to invite a guest
> Hello [name], I host [show name], a podcast for [who]. I really liked [something specific they did or said]. I'd love to have you on for about 30 minutes to talk about [topic]. We can record online at a time that suits you, and I'll send the questions first. Would you be open to it?

### Guest release (read and agree before recording)
> "Do you agree that this conversation can be recorded, edited and published as a podcast, and used in short clips to promote the show?"

Record the guest's "yes" at the start of the recording.

## Part 4: Record and edit
1. Do a test recording and listen on earphones.
2. Keep going if you make a mistake: pause, clap once, then say the sentence again. The clap is easy to spot when editing.
3. For remote interviews, use a recording tool that saves each person's audio separately. Ask your guest to wear earphones.
4. Edit with a free tool: Audacity on a laptop, or a mobile audio editor on your phone.
5. Cut long silences, false starts and off-topic parts. Don't over-edit: people like natural speech.
6. Keep voice levels even from start to finish.
7. Export as MP3.

**Music warning:** don't use popular songs, even for a few seconds. Your episode can be removed and you can face a copyright claim. Use music you've paid for, royalty-free music with a licence that allows podcasts, or music made for you.

## Part 5: Publish
1. **Pick a podcast host.** This is where your audio lives and it creates your RSS feed. Compare current prices and limits before choosing. Options include Spotify for Creators, Buzzsprout, RSS.com and Africa-focused hosts such as Wokpa and Afripods.
2. **Make your cover art.** A square image, 3000 × 3000 pixels, with a short, readable title. Check that it's still clear when shrunk to the size of a thumbnail.
3. **Write your show description.** Use your one-line pitch, then two sentences on what listeners get. Use the words people would search for.
4. **Submit your RSS feed** to Apple Podcasts, Spotify and any other apps your audience uses. Apps can take from a few hours to a few days to list a new show.
5. **Launch with three episodes** so new listeners have something to stay for.
6. **Send your show to DoyinTech Listen** for a free listing: doyintech.com/listen

## Part 6: Launch checklist
**Two weeks before**
- [ ] Show name, one-line pitch and cover art are ready
- [ ] Three episodes are recorded and edited
- [ ] Host account is set up
- [ ] Next two guests are booked

**Launch week**
- [ ] Publish three episodes
- [ ] Submit the feed to the main podcast apps
- [ ] Post on WhatsApp Status, Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn and X
- [ ] Personally message 20 people who would enjoy it
- [ ] Ask guests to share their episode, and send them a ready-made clip

**Every episode**
- [ ] Cut a 30–60 second clip for social media
- [ ] Write a title that says what the listener gets
- [ ] Write show notes with links and a summary
- [ ] Share in the groups where your listeners already spend time, if the group allows it
- [ ] Reply to every comment and message

## Part 7: Good episode titles
Say what the listener will get. Compare:
- Weak: "Episode 12 with Tolu"
- Strong: "How Tolu Got Her First 100 Customers With Only WhatsApp"

**Formulas**
- How [person] did [result] without [common problem]
- [Number] mistakes [audience] make with [topic]
- What I wish I knew before [big step]
- The truth about [popular belief]
- [Topic] explained in [time]

## Part 8: Earn from your show
Most podcasts take time to earn. Plan to spend your first months building trust and a small, loyal audience. Your show can often earn before it's big if you start with the ideas near the top of this list.

1. **Sell your own service or product.** A coach, lawyer, baker or consultant can win clients from a small audience who already trust them.
2. **Sell a digital product:** a guide, template pack, course or paid community.
3. **Local sponsors.** A business that wants your exact audience can be a better fit than a big brand. Offer a simple package: a 30-second read in four episodes, plus one social post.
4. **Listener support.** Offer bonus episodes or a thank-you in the show, paid through a link.
5. **Live events and workshops.** Your listeners can become your first ticket buyers.
6. **Video.** Film your recording and publish it on YouTube too.

**Sponsor message template**
> Hello [name], I host [show name], a podcast for [who]. We publish every [day] and reach [real number] listeners per episode. Our listeners are [describe them], which is why I thought of [brand]. I'd like to offer you [package] for [price] for one month. Could I send you more details?

Always use your real numbers, and tell listeners when something is sponsored.
